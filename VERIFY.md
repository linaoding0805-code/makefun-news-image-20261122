## 2. Lead — Dymer strike: "A Russian drone brings down a five-storey block in Dymer, north of Kyiv — 14 missing at first light, three bodies found; six killed across the Kyiv region. Zelenskyy counts 165 missiles and 2,700 drones in a week and presses Washington for Patriots." Caption: "a child was pulled out alive on Sunday evening."

**Verdict: PASS** (with notes on figures that moved later in the day)

- **Five-storey block in Dymer destroyed by a Russian drone:** confirmed. Reuters ("At least 14 people missing after Russian drone hit building in Kyiv region", 22 Nov 2026); AP via Military.com; AFP via South China Morning Post / sh.itjust.works. The strike hit at ~05:00 on Sunday morning, destroying a section from the first to the fifth floor and starting a fire.
- **"14 missing at first light":** confirmed as the morning figure. President Zelenskyy posted on X on Sunday morning that "14 people remain unaccounted for" and that nine people had been evacuated (Reuters; Kyiv Independent via Europe Says; Washington Examiner). Note: Ukrainian police later listed **15** missing, including two children (an 11-year-old girl and a 7-year-old boy) — the entry's "14" is the morning figure and is accurate as stated, but a later edition would use 15.
- **"Three bodies found":** confirmed. AFP / Ukraine's State Emergency Service: "The bodies of three victims have been recovered from the rubble," including a woman born in 1978 and a teenager born in 2008 (AFP via South China Morning Post, reported in The Library Estate's live file; Sweden Herald).
- **"Six killed across the Kyiv region":** confirmed. AFP: Sunday's toll across the Kyiv region was at least six — three in Dymer, two in Vyshneve (grocery-store strike) and one in Bila Tserkva district (AFP via The Library Estate; BBC: "The attack early on Sunday morning in the village of Dymer killed six people" per regional head Tymur Tkachenko). Note: the Dymer toll alone later rose (5 by Sunday evening per UNN, 7 by early Monday per the Kyiv regional administration) — the entry reflects the Sunday daytime accounting and is not wrong, but the figure was already moving.
- **"165 missiles and 2,700 drones in a week":** confirmed by two independent sources. Kyiv Independent (via Europe Says, "Ukraine war latest", 22 Nov): "Russia launched around 2,700 attack drones, nearly 1,460 glide bombs, and 165 missiles of various types against Ukraine this week"; Washington Examiner (22 Nov) reports the same three figures from Zelenskyy's Sunday post. The entry omits the ~1,460 glide bombs — an omission, not an error.
- **"Presses Washington for Patriots":** confirmed. Zelenskyy called the delays in delivering Patriot interceptors "difficult to understand" and said it was "very important" the US authorise transfer of missiles already paid for by partners under NATO's PURL programme (Kyiv Independent; Washington Examiner; Politico via Daily Guardian).
- **Caption — "a child was pulled out alive on Sunday evening":** confirmed. BBC: a 7-year-old boy was pulled alive from the rubble 14 hours after the strike; The Gaze (citing Ukraine's Interior Ministry and Zelenskyy): the boy, Davyd/David, was found under collapsed concrete after ~14 hours, is in stable condition, and Zelenskyy confirmed the rescue in his Sunday evening address ("This evening, a child was rescued from under the rubble in Dymer"). AFP likewise: "On Sunday evening, rescuers managed to pull out a child alive."

**Correction needed:** none required. Optional note: if the edition is re-set, update "14 missing" to the later police figure of 15 and note the Dymer toll rose after the Sunday daytime count.

## 5. "BepiColombo reaches Mercury" — "After eight years, the ESA-JAXA probe slips into orbit — though a thruster glitch cut its braking burn short."

**Verdict: FAIL — wrong date (event was Saturday 21 November, not Sunday 22 November)**

- **The orbit insertion happened on Saturday 21 November 2026, not Sunday.** ESA's own page ("BepiColombo entering orbit around Mercury", esa.int) and multiple outlets agree: the four-thruster braking burn fired at 18:24 UTC (19:24 CET) on **21 November**, a 37-minute burn, with ESA flight controllers confirming capture several hours later. Nature (21 Nov 2026): "On 21 November, the European Space Agency (ESA) announced that a European-built module had inserted itself into Mercury's orbit." EarthSky: "went into orbit around Mercury yesterday (November 21, 2026)". Wikipedia's BepiColombo entry lists "Orbital insertion: 21 November 2026."
- **"After eight years"** is correct (launched 20 October 2018).
- **The thruster glitch is real — but it was first disclosed on Sunday, which is the only 22 November element of this story.** Reuters (22 Nov): "ESA confirmed on Saturday that BepiColombo had begun its orbit. In a social media post on Sunday, ESA said the spacecraft had a successful entry into orbit, but during the maneuver, 'the thruster burn was shorter than planned following a software issue.'" Wikipedia: "The thruster burn was shorter than the expected 37 minutes due to a software issue. However, the spacecraft stack remained safe and entered an orbit around the planet."
- The entry as written presents the *arrival itself* ("reaches Mercury", "slips into orbit") as Sunday news. That is a date error: the arrival was Saturday's news.

**Correction needed (blocking):** Reframe as a Sunday follow-up and re-date the event, e.g.: "BepiColombo in Mercury orbit — ESA says Sunday its braking burn ran short after a software glitch; the ESA-JAXA probe was captured into orbit on Saturday after an eight-year journey." Do not present the insertion as a 22 November event.

### (c) Boat carrying mourners capsizes in Nigeria — "at least 24 dead"

**Verdict: FAIL — facts correct, but this is not 22 November news (event Friday 20 Nov; toll confirmed Saturday 21 Nov)**

- **The facts are confirmed:** AP (via St Paul Daily News / Greensboro Times): "A boat carrying people to a funeral has capsized in southern Nigeria, killing at least 24 people, emergency services said on Saturday"; the boat sank on **Friday** in Bayelsa State while taking passengers from Ayama to Ukubie for a burial ceremony. NEMA spokesman Manzo Ezekiel confirmed 24 bodies recovered and 24 survivors rescued. People: President Tinubu announced the incident on Saturday 21 Nov, saying it occurred "a day prior" (Friday).
- **Date problem:** the capsizing was Friday 20 November and the "at least 24" toll was first confirmed by NEMA on Saturday 21 November. It was neither an event of Sunday 22 November nor first reported that day. (One follow-up, GSM Global News, is dated Sunday 22 Nov, but it is explicitly a follow-up to its 21 Nov report — the toll itself is Saturday's news.) Listing it under "ALSO TODAY" on a 22 November front page misdates it by a day.

**Correction needed (blocking):** drop the item from the 22 November edition, or replace it with a genuine Sunday story. Do not carry it as "also today" news for 22 November.
